\title{More Data, Worse Water: When Expanding a Species Distribution Model's
Training Extent Degrades Skill on the Region It Serves}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Deepak Krishna}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{MarisAI} \\
India \\
deepakkrishna2206@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
More training data is conventionally assumed to help a species distribution
model. We report a controlled counter-example: expanding a fish-habitat
ensemble's training extent from one regional sea to the global ocean
multiplies training rows \DataMultiple{}$\times$ (\NRegionalTrain{} to
\NGlobalTrain{}) without extending the usable label window, and on
\NHoldout{} identical held-out regional rows --- the global model's training
set with the holdout's spatial blocks removed, to prevent leakage --- every
accuracy metric is worse: true skill statistic \TSSGlobal{} against
\TSSRegion{}, Boyce index \BoyceGlobal{} against \BoyceRegion{}. A comparative
Multivariate Environmental Similarity Surface (MESS) analysis rules out the
obvious explanation: the global model extrapolates \emph{less} on this
holdout than the regional model does on its own (\MESSFracGlobal{}\% of rows
outside its training envelope, against \MESSFracRegion{}\%). SHAP feature
attribution instead shows a mechanism: the regional model's leading features
are coastal geometry (depth, distance to coast); the global model's are
water-mass chemistry (dissolved oxygen, 60-day lagged temperature), which
displaces the fine, locally decisive signal with coarser variables that
generalise across oceans but say less about any one of them. An ablation confirms the mechanism causally rather than leaving it a SHAP-only association: removing the two displaced features from the global LightGBM tier improves every metric on the same holdout, closing \AblGapClosedPct{}\% of that tier's gap to the regional model. We reconcile
this with the two closest prior findings, one of which recommends
range-wide data despite finding it does not help \citep{elgabbas2018}, and
one of which recommends larger training extents on an apparently opposite
finding \citep{hanberry2026}, showing both address a different axis of
variation than the one manipulated here.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Reproducibility}
The matched-holdout comparison is built by
\texttt{machine\_learning/scripts/validate\_global\_on\_region.py}, which
reproduces the regional model's own final holdout split, removes the holdout's
spatial blocks from the global feature store, refits both ensembles with the
shared training routine, and scores both on the identical holdout rows,
writing \texttt{machine\_learning/reports/global\_vs\_regional\_habitat.csv}.
SHAP importances are read from
\texttt{machine\_learning/reports/fish\_habitat\_summary.json} (regional) and
\texttt{fish\_habitat\_global\_ocean\_summary.json} (global). The ablation is
\texttt{machine\_learning/scripts/ablate\_global\_chemistry\_features.py}, writing
\texttt{machine\_learning/reports/global\_ablation\_chemistry\_features.csv}.
Every number in this paper was verified against those files as of \AsOf{}.
