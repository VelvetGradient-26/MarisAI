\title{Rising Skill, Falling Skill: Why a Single Baseline Cannot Tell You
Whether an Ocean Forecast Model Has Learned Anything}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Deepak Krishna}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{MarisAI} \\
India \\
deepakkrishna2206@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Statistical forecasts of ocean state are conventionally scored against
\emph{persistence}, the last observed value. We show this single baseline is
not merely incomplete but actively misleading, because it fails in a direction
that flatters the model. Persistence decays with forecast horizon while a
seasonal cycle does not, so a model that has learned nothing but the time of
year exhibits skill against persistence that \emph{increases} with horizon ---
the signature normally read as evidence of a strong model. We audit \NVars{}
operational ocean variables across \NSites{} globally distributed sites and
four horizons (\SIrange{1}{30}{\day}), using one gradient-boosted framework
with no variable-specific code, and score every model against both persistence
and a leakage-controlled day-of-year climatology. Median skill against
persistence rises from \MedSkillPOne{} at \SI{1}{\day} to \MedSkillPThirty{} at
\SI{30}{\day}; over the same range median skill against climatology falls from
\MedSkillCOne{} to \MedSkillCThirty{}. The two baselines therefore support
opposite conclusions about the same models, and \NLoseToClim{} model--horizon
combinations beat persistence while losing to climatology outright. We further
test whether these globally pooled models transfer to locations they never saw,
using leave-one-site-out validation blocked in space and time simultaneously
(\NLosoFits{} held-out fits). Transfer is real but costly: median held-out
skill at \SI{7}{\day} is \MedLosoSeven{} against \MedPooledSeven{} pooled, with
\LosoPositiveShare{}\% of held-out sites retaining positive skill. We report
\NLoseToPers{} model--horizon combinations that lose to persistence, and argue
that reporting both baselines across multiple horizons should be a minimum
standard for this class of work.
\end{abstract}
