# Agentic AI-Powered Marine Intelligence Platform

## Background

The marine ecosystem plays a vital role in supporting livelihoods, food security, biodiversity, maritime transportation, coastal resilience, and the blue economy. Every day, vast volumes of satellite Earth Observation and oceanographic data, including Sea Surface Temperature (SST), chlorophyll concentration, and weather forecasts, are generated by ISRO and other global agencies.

Marine stakeholders such as fishermen, researchers, coastal authorities, disaster management agencies, and maritime operators rely on timely access to oceanographic and meteorological information for operational planning and decision-making. As the volume, diversity, and complexity of marine data continue to increase, there is a growing need for an intelligent conversational platform that enables users to interact naturally with marine information, ask questions, explore scenarios, and receive synthesized, evidence-based recommendations tailored to their context.

Advances in Agentic AI, conversational intelligence, and geospatial technologies present an opportunity to fundamentally transform how marine information is accessed and utilized. By integrating satellite Earth Observation data with autonomous AI agents, intelligent conversational decision-support systems should be able to answer questions such as:

- "Where is the nearest Potential Fishing Zone today?"
- "Is it safe to venture into the sea tomorrow morning?"
- "What are the tide, weather, and sea conditions near my fishing location?"

The system should provide explainable, context-aware recommendations based on relevant marine and environmental information.

## Description

Develop an **Agentic AI-powered conversational platform** that enables users to access, analyze, and reason over marine information using natural language.

The platform should autonomously:

1. Interpret user intent.
2. Decompose complex requests into executable tasks.
3. Coordinate multiple specialized AI agents.
4. Retrieve relevant marine and geospatial datasets.
5. Perform spatial-temporal reasoning.
6. Correlate information across heterogeneous data sources.
7. Synthesize actionable recommendations through a conversational interface.

The solution should be capable of integrating information from multiple sources, including:

- Satellite Earth Observation products
- GIS layers
- Weather services
- Oceanographic observations
- Marine advisories
- Other relevant public-domain marine and environmental datasets

### Typical User Queries

The platform should support queries such as:

- Where is the nearest Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) today?
- Is it safe to venture into the sea tomorrow morning?
- What are the tide, weather, and sea conditions near my fishing location?
- Are there any lightning or cyclone alerts in my area?
- Which regions show high chlorophyll concentration and favourable sea surface temperature?
- What is the safest route for a fishing vessel considering weather and sea-state conditions?
- Why has fish productivity declined in a particular coastal region?
- Which fishing zones should be avoided due to hazardous marine conditions or geofencing restrictions?

The platform should **not merely retrieve information from individual datasets**. It should intelligently correlate observations from multiple sources, explain the reasoning behind its recommendations, and present insights through:

- Conversational responses
- Interactive maps
- Charts
- Geospatial visualizations
- Alerts
- Marine advisories
- Evidence-backed recommendations

## Expected Solution

Participants are expected to develop an **Agentic AI-powered Marine Intelligence Platform** that leverages collaborative AI agents, geospatial technologies, and satellite Earth Observation data to provide intelligent conversational decision support.

The solution should demonstrate the core principles of **Agentic AI**, including:

- Autonomous planning
- Reasoning
- Tool selection
- Task execution
- Collaboration among specialized agents
- Explainable decision-making

### Core Capabilities

The platform should be capable of:

#### 1. Natural Language Understanding

- Understand user intent expressed in natural language.
- Convert conversational requests into structured tasks.
- Handle ambiguous queries using context and clarification when necessary.

#### 2. Multilingual Conversational Intelligence

- Automatically identify the language of the user's query.
- Respond in the same language as the user.
- Provide strong support for Indian regional languages.

#### 3. Contextual Multi-Turn Conversations

- Maintain conversational context across multiple turns.
- Allow users to refine previous queries.
- Enable users to explore related scenarios and follow-up questions without repeating information.

#### 4. Autonomous Data Discovery and Retrieval

- Discover relevant datasets based on the user's request.
- Retrieve and integrate satellite, marine, meteorological, and geospatial information.
- Select appropriate data sources and tools autonomously.

#### 5. Spatial, Temporal, and Contextual Reasoning

- Correlate observations from multiple heterogeneous data sources.
- Perform spatial analysis based on user location, vessel location, coastal regions, fishing zones, and operational boundaries.
- Perform temporal analysis using historical, current, and forecast information.
- Combine environmental variables to derive meaningful marine intelligence.

#### 6. Explainable Decision Support

- Generate evidence-based recommendations.
- Clearly communicate why a recommendation was made.
- Provide the supporting datasets, observations, advisories, and reasoning used to derive each recommendation.
- Distinguish between observed conditions, forecasts, derived insights, and recommendations.

#### 7. Fishermen Safety

Enhance maritime safety through proactive alerts for:

- Adverse weather
- High waves
- Strong winds
- Lightning
- Cyclones
- Dangerous sea-state conditions
- Other hazardous marine conditions

#### 8. Geofencing and Boundary Awareness

Provide notifications when a vessel or user approaches:

- International maritime boundaries
- Restricted waters
- Marine protected areas
- Ecologically sensitive zones
- No-fishing zones
- Other predefined operational boundaries

#### 9. Route Optimization and Navigation Support

Assist with:

- Safe route planning
- Route optimization
- Navigation support
- Operational planning

Routes should consider prevailing and forecast:

- Weather conditions
- Wind
- Wave height
- Sea state
- Other relevant marine hazards

#### 10. Reliable Recommendations and Evidence

Every significant recommendation should be accompanied by:

- Supporting evidence
- Relevant data sources
- Key observations
- Reasoning or factors considered
- Appropriate uncertainty or confidence information where applicable

## Suggested Multi-Agent Architecture

Participants are encouraged to design a **modular multi-agent architecture** comprising specialized AI agents.

Potential specialized agents include:

| Agent | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Planning Agent** | Understands user objectives, decomposes complex requests, and creates execution plans |
| **Marine Data Discovery Agent** | Identifies and retrieves relevant marine and Earth Observation datasets |
| **Weather Intelligence Agent** | Analyzes weather forecasts, warnings, wind, rainfall, lightning, and cyclone information |
| **Ocean Analytics Agent** | Processes SST, chlorophyll, waves, currents, tides, and other oceanographic parameters |
| **Geospatial Reasoning Agent** | Performs spatial queries, proximity analysis, geofencing, overlays, and route-related reasoning |
| **Risk Assessment Agent** | Evaluates marine hazards and generates contextual risk assessments |
| **Visualization Agent** | Converts analytical outputs into maps, charts, layers, and interactive geospatial visualizations |
| **Reporting Agent** | Synthesizes findings, evidence, and recommendations into clear reports or responses |
| **User Interaction Agent** | Handles conversational interaction, language detection, context, and user-facing responses |

### Agent Collaboration

The architecture should demonstrate autonomous collaboration among agents to solve complex marine intelligence problems.

For example, a query such as:

> "Is it safe for me to go fishing tomorrow morning, and where is the nearest good fishing zone?"

could trigger a workflow in which:

1. The **User Interaction Agent** identifies the user's intent, language, and location.
2. The **Planning Agent** decomposes the request into safety assessment and fishing-zone discovery tasks.
3. The **Weather Intelligence Agent** retrieves forecast weather, wind, lightning, cyclone, and rainfall information.
4. The **Ocean Analytics Agent** retrieves and analyzes SST, chlorophyll, wave, current, and other oceanographic parameters.
5. The **Geospatial Reasoning Agent** identifies nearby PFZs and checks restricted or protected areas.
6. The **Risk Assessment Agent** evaluates the combined marine conditions.
7. The **Visualization Agent** prepares a map showing the user's location, recommended fishing zones, hazards, and boundaries.
8. The **Reporting Agent** synthesizes the evidence and produces an explainable recommendation.
9. The **User Interaction Agent** communicates the result conversationally in the user's preferred language.

## Key Design Principles

The proposed solution should emphasize:

- **Agentic autonomy** — agents should independently plan, select tools, execute tasks, and collaborate.
- **Data fusion** — insights should be derived by correlating multiple marine and environmental datasets.
- **Spatial intelligence** — location, proximity, boundaries, routes, and geospatial relationships should be first-class components.
- **Temporal intelligence** — current observations, forecasts, and historical trends should be considered where relevant.
- **Explainability** — recommendations should clearly communicate the evidence and reasoning behind them.
- **Reliability** — the system should prioritize authoritative data sources and communicate uncertainty appropriately.
- **Multilingual accessibility** — the platform should be usable by diverse Indian coastal communities.
- **Human-centered interaction** — complex marine information should be translated into simple, actionable conversational guidance.
- **Interactive visualization** — maps and other visual interfaces should complement conversational responses.
- **Modularity and extensibility** — new agents, datasets, tools, and services should be easy to integrate.

---

## Overall Objective

The ultimate goal is to transform complex and fragmented marine information into an **intelligent, conversational, explainable, and actionable Marine Intelligence Platform**.

The platform should bridge the gap between large-scale satellite and oceanographic data and real-world decision-making by enabling fishermen, researchers, coastal authorities, disaster management agencies, and maritime operators to **ask natural-language questions and receive context-aware answers, visual insights, safety alerts, and evidence-based recommendations.**
